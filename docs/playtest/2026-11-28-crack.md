# Playtest: the crack prototype (2026-11-28, Claude)

One game played by Claude through `--plain`, before the maintainer's first
playtest. One game is an anecdote, not data.

## Build

Branch `claude/gallant-allen-x0xidg`. `carrier crack --seed 31337`.

## The game

| # | Guess | Feedback | What it told me |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `X R Z .` | 1 HIT 2 NEAR | X, R and Z are all in the code; one is in place |
| 2 | `R X Z E` | 0 HIT 3 NEAR | E is out; Z is not in slot 3, so X is slot 1 or R is slot 2 |
| 3 | `X Z R A` | 3 HIT 0 NEAR | Slots 1 to 3 solved; A is out |
| 4 | `X Z R K` | 4 HIT | ACCESS GRANTED, alarm 40 |

## Questions (addendum 03, §4)

1. **Fun?** The deduction is satisfying: each count narrows things down,
   and the swaps felt like fiddling with a lock. Turn 3, where two swaps and
   one new card tested a whole theory, was the best moment.
2. **Energy 3?** It never limited me. A good guess changes one to three
   slots, and that is what 3 energy buys.
3. **Hand getting in the way?** No. With 12 cards, 2 of each symbol, I
   always held the symbol I wanted.
4. **10 guesses?** Far too many for a careful player: 4 used. The first
   guess was lucky (3 of 3 symbols in the code).
5. **Counts or per-slot marks?** Counts were readable at 4 slots. Untested
   at 5 or more.

## What felt wrong

- **Too easy, and the cards don't matter yet.** Every card is the same kind
  of thing, and the deck always had what I needed. The puzzle was pure
  Mastermind; the deck added nothing.
- **Hand numbers shift after each placement.** Placing hand card 1 renumbers
  the rest, so `1 1`, `1 2`, `1 3` places the first three cards. It works,
  but it is easy to get wrong.
- **Nothing pushes back.** The server only counts. With no pressure but the
  alarm, a careful player never feels at risk.

## Decisions

None yet; these wait for the maintainer's playtest. Candidates: fewer
guesses (alarm +15 or +20), a thinner deck or more symbols so the hand
matters, and the first server interference (rotate, scramble).
